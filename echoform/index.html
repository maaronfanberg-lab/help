How to scan & what this can actually show
  1. Open over HTTPS in Safari, and enable the microphone.
  2. Disconnect headphones and use the iPhone's built-in speaker. Press Speaker test and check whether sound actually comes out.
  3. Press Ping once. You should hear a short chirp, see the live input waveform and get a measured echo profile.
  4. If chirp detection fails, try the 3–7.5 kHz band, face a wall, reduce room noise and ping again.
  5. Calibrate reference to capture a comparison acoustic signature. This is not a geometry or hardware calibration.
  6. Press Use phone motion or adjust the angle manually, and rotate the phone between pings. Radar dots show measured delays at your scanning angle, not independently known directions.
  7. Export scan saves only local calculated data. No microphone recordings are sent to a server.

Large soft objects absorb sound, multiple paths confuse ranging, and the direct speaker-to-mic path can hide close objects. Do not use this prototype for safety-critical navigation.

MEASUREMENT, NOT MAGIC. Uses real microphone capture and chirp matched filtering. A green mic status means capture is active, not that the range estimate is correct. Room reconstruction from one phone is fundamentally underdetermined. iOS Safari: HTTPS + permission required.
